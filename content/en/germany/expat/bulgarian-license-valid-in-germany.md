**Update (November 2026):** In the first version of this post I wrote that converting a Turkish licence into a Bulgarian one doesn't help in Germany. In August 2026 the German rules changed, and I exchanged my Bulgarian licence for a German one **without any test**. I have rewritten the post to reflect that.

## The short answer

Since 18 August 2026, Germany recognises a valid driving licence issued by another EU country **regardless of which non-EU country the original licence came from.** So if you exchanged your Turkish licence for an EU licence in Bulgaria (or another EU country), it is now valid in Germany and can be exchanged for a German licence without tests.

The change was made in section 28 of the German Driving Licence Regulation (FeV). Before, EU licences obtained by exchanging a non-EU licence without a test were not recognised in Germany unless the original country was on a specific list. Turkey is not on that list, so Bulgarian licences of Turkish origin were refused.

*Note: This is my own experience, not legal advice. Check with your local driving licence office (Führerscheinstelle) before you act.*

## My story

My father's family were Bulgarian emigrants. I received Bulgarian citizenship in August 2024 and moved to Germany in February 2025. My Turkish licence was only valid for six months in Germany. Since I had a registered address in Bulgaria, I decided to exchange my Turkish licence for a Bulgarian one.

**The exchange in Bulgaria:** I got the medical certificate the same day at a small clinic in Kardzhali. The hard part was the licensing offices in Sofia (KAT): everything was in Bulgarian, the officers didn't speak English, and my first attempts failed. When I came back with a Turkish–Bulgarian interpreter, the application went through quickly. About 30 days later my Bulgarian licence was ready. A code on the back showed it had originally been exchanged from a Turkish licence.

**First refusal in Germany:** At the licensing office in Munich the answer was clear: this licence is not valid in Germany because it originates from Turkey. For Germany I still had a Turkish licence and needed the full process: eye test, first-aid course, theory and practical test. I signed up with a driving school and paid the first instalment.

**October 2026:** When I went to the KVR to apply, they told me the rule had changed in August, that I no longer needed any test, and that I could exchange my Bulgarian licence directly. I applied on 5 October and had my German licence on **22 October**. I cancelled my driving school contract.

## Exchanging in Bulgaria: what you need to know

- **You need a registered address in Bulgaria.** Without it you cannot apply.
- **Bring an interpreter.** Without Bulgarian it is almost impossible to get through the KAT office alone.
- **Documents:** Bulgarian ID or passport, original Turkish licence and a copy, medical certificate, biometric photo. Some officers ask for a sworn Bulgarian translation of the Turkish licence; ask in advance.
- **You hand in your Turkish licence** during the exchange.

## Exchanging in Germany

- Book an appointment at your licensing office (KVR in Munich) and say you want to exchange an EU licence.
- If you were refused before for this reason, you can apply again; the old refusal was based on the old rule.
- The change does not cover forged or improperly obtained licences.

## If you only have a Turkish licence

With a Turkish licence you can drive in Germany for **six months** from your residence registration (Anmeldung). After that you need an eye test, a first-aid course and **theory and practical tests** to get a German licence. The new rule only applies to licences exchanged in another EU country. See our [guide to driving in Germany with a Turkish licence](/en/germany/expat/driving-license-renewal-germany/).

## In short

My first attempt made the Bulgarian route look like a waste of time. With the August 2026 change, the same route became the shortest way to a German licence without tests. Rules can change again, so check with your licensing office first.
