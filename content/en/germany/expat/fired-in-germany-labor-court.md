Being fired in Germany was both a shock and a bureaucratic marathon for me: a termination by email, the labour court, the Jobcenter and a settlement. Here is what happened and what I wish I had known earlier.

*Note: This is not legal advice, just my own experience. For your own case, talk to a lawyer or the advice desk at the labour court.*

## The termination came by email, and that is not valid

I found out I was being let go by email. In Germany a termination (**Kündigung**) must be **in writing, on paper, with a handwritten signature.** Email, fax or a scanned document is not a legally valid termination.

This detail shaped everything that followed. Without a signed paper termination, I could not file a claim for a while, and the Jobcenter wanted to see what was happening with the court case. In the end I started my benefits with a letter documenting this. The company later printed, signed and posted the termination, and the process continued from there.

## The deadline to sue is short: 3 weeks

Once you receive a valid written termination, you usually have only **3 weeks** to file an unfair dismissal claim (**Kündigungsschutzklage**). If you miss it, the termination can stand even if it was unfair.

Something I didn't know and found very useful: you don't need a lawyer to file. Labour courts (**Arbeitsgericht**) have an advice and filing desk (**Rechtsantragstelle**) where you can file your claim free of charge.

## Court: first a conciliation hearing

The first hearing is a conciliation hearing (**Güteverhandlung**). The judge listens to both sides and tries to reach an agreement. That is what happened in my case: the employer offered a settlement, I asked for more, and we agreed somewhere in between.

If your German isn't good enough, you can ask for an interpreter. There was one at my hearing.

## Two mistakes I made

**1. I had no legal insurance.** One consultation with a lawyer cost me €220. At the first instance in German labour courts, **each side pays its own lawyer, whether you win or lose.** A longer case could easily have cost thousands. That makes legal expenses insurance (**Rechtsschutzversicherung**) very important here. But note: most policies have a waiting period and won't cover a problem that started before you signed up. Get it as soon as you start working.

**2. The payment date wasn't in the court record.** The settlement amount was recorded, but not the payment date. The company delayed payment, and I only got the money after saying I would restart the process. If you settle, **put the payment date in the record too.**

## Settlement and the Jobcenter

At the hearing I asked whether the settlement would affect my benefits and was told it wouldn't. But the Jobcenter counted it as income, **spread it over six months**, and reduced my benefits accordingly. We appealed; nothing changed. If you receive Jobcenter benefits, expect much of a settlement to be deducted.

## What I learned

- A termination must be **signed and on paper**; email is not valid.
- You usually have **3 weeks** to file. No lawyer? Go to the labour court's filing desk.
- Register as a jobseeker with the Agentur für Arbeit as soon as you learn you will lose your job.
- Get **legal insurance** before you need it.
- If you settle, record the **payment date**.
- On Jobcenter benefits, a settlement may be largely deducted.
