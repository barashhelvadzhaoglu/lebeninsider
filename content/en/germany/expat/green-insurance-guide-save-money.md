When we moved to Munich by car, one of the first "small but important" issues was the green card insurance. In Turkey we were quoted about **€120** for three months. In the end we paid **€45** for the same period at the Bulgarian border. Here is how we did it, and when this works and when it doesn't.

## What is green card insurance?

Your Turkish compulsory car insurance is not valid abroad. To drive in Europe you need insurance under the **Green Card** system. There are two ways to get it:

- **In Turkey, from your insurer:** covers most Green Card countries, including non-EU countries such as Serbia. More expensive.
- **At the Bulgarian border:** after Kapıkule there are small insurance offices on the Bulgarian side. Policies sold there usually cover **EU countries only** and are much cheaper.

## What we did

We found the €120 quote in Turkey too high. After some research and asking people who had done the trip, we decided to buy at the border.

After crossing Kapıkule, we found the insurance offices on the Bulgarian side near the truck waiting area. The staff understood basic Turkish. We handed over the registration and passport, and they said: "90 days, EU countries, 45 euros." We paid in cash and got the policy. It took 15–20 minutes and saved us about **€75**.

The policy really did list only EU countries, so we planned our route through **Romania and Hungary** instead of Serbia. Romania's roads turned out to be much better than we expected.

## Things to check

1. **Does your route leave the EU?** If you drive through Serbia or North Macedonia, an EU-only border policy won't cover you. Either buy a full green card in Turkey or keep your route inside the EU.
2. **Carry cash in euros.** The border offices usually want cash.
3. **Check the policy before you pay.** Plate, chassis number, name, dates and the list of covered countries.
4. **Prices change.** The amounts above are from our own trip. Check current prices before you leave.
5. **Plan the dates.** Before the policy expires, the car must have left the country or be insured another way. How long you may drive in Germany with a Turkish licence is a separate question; think about both together.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I see which countries the green card covers?**
The card shows boxes with country codes. Countries whose box is crossed out are not covered. Check every country on your route before you leave.

**Does green card insurance replace comprehensive cover?**
No. It is third-party liability insurance only. For damage to your own car, ask your insurer whether your comprehensive policy (kasko) covers you abroad.

**What if we have an accident on the way?**
Carry a European Accident Statement form. You fill it in together with the other driver; the layout is the same in every language, so it works even if you don't share a language.

**Can I buy the policy online in advance?**
We bought ours at the border. Some Bulgarian insurers sell online too, but the border offices handle it quickly and in cash.

**What happens once I'm in Germany?**
Green card insurance is for the journey and temporary stays. Once you live in Germany, keeping a Turkish-registered car long term is not practical; learn the registration, insurance and customs rules in advance. We later sold our car in Turkey.

## In short

If your whole route is inside the EU, buying green card insurance at the Bulgarian border saves real money. We saved about €75 on a three-month policy. If you pass through a non-EU country, you need a full policy from Turkey.
