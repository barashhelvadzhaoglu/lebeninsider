Before moving to Munich, I seriously looked at three countries: **the Netherlands, Luxembourg and Germany.** I applied for jobs, had interviews and compared what life would look like for our family in each. In the end we chose Germany. Here is how I compared them and why Germany won for us.

This is not a ranking. Each country is the right choice for someone. These were our priorities, as a family with two young children.

## What we compared

1. **Net income after rent**, not gross salary.
2. **Housing:** can a family realistically find a home?
3. **Healthcare and childcare** for the children.
4. **Language and community:** how hard daily life would be, and whether there was a Turkish community.
5. **Long-term stability:** job market size and how secure we would feel in ten years.

## The Netherlands

**What attracted us:** strong salaries in tech, English is widely spoken at work and in daily life, and the well-known tax benefit for incoming skilled workers (the 30% ruling, which is being reduced to 27% from 2027).

**What held us back:** the housing shortage, especially around Amsterdam and the Randstad, where finding a family home is very hard. Every adult buys health insurance individually and pays a yearly deductible (children are covered free), so for a family with one income it is a noticeable cost. And the tax benefit is temporary; you have to plan for life after it ends.

## Luxembourg

**What attracted us:** low taxes compared with its neighbours, high salaries, and free public transport for everyone. Around half of the population are foreign nationals, so being a foreigner is completely normal.

**What held us back:** housing is extremely expensive and scarce, which cancels much of the tax advantage for a family. The country is small, so the job market in our field was limited. Many people working in Luxembourg live across the border in France, Belgium or Germany and commute every day.

## Germany

**What attracted us:** the largest job market in Europe, so if one job ends there is another. Statutory health insurance covers a non-working spouse and children at no extra cost. Childcare is subsidised, in some states free for certain age groups. There is a large Turkish community, so familiar food, shops and people are never far away. And everyday life is orderly and safe for children.

**What held us back:** German. In Munich you can work in English, but bureaucracy, schools and doctors often require German. Rents in Munich are high, and the bureaucracy is slow.

## Why Germany won

For our family, the decisive factors were **stability, healthcare and childcare, and the size of the job market.** Luxembourg's low taxes and free transport were tempting, but the cost of family housing and the small job market weighed more heavily. The Netherlands offered good salaries and English, but the housing shortage and the temporary nature of the tax benefit made us hesitate.

Germany was the hardest to enter because of the language, but it felt like the place where we could build a long-term life.

## What I would tell you

- **Compare net income after rent**, not gross salaries.
- **Check family housing** in the city you are considering before accepting an offer.
- **Start learning the local language early**, even if work is in English.
- **Think in ten-year terms:** where will your children go to school, and what happens if you lose your job?
