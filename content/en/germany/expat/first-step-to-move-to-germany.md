If you are unhappy with your life in Turkey and thinking about moving abroad, especially to Germany, this post is for you.

I have just one piece of advice: **start today.** Not tomorrow, not "once everything is ready." Let me explain why through my own experience.

## Why today?

Because the waiting time is very long. Here is what we experienced:

- **Job search visa:** I submitted my application. The email inviting me to an appointment arrived about **a year later**: "Click the link below if you will attend the appointment."
- **Family reunification:** Later, I also applied for a family reunification appointment via iDATA. The invitation email for my wife arrived about **9–10 months later**.

In other words, there can be a full year between the day you apply and the day you actually set foot in the consulate. That year doesn't have to go to waste; but if you don't submit the application, that waiting period never even begins.

## My story

Our path to moving was actually different: we moved using our Bulgarian citizenship. However, that citizenship process took **more than ten years**. We had no idea when or even if it would conclude. Because of that, we couldn't rely on it completely.

When the citizenship process dragged on again in 2024, I thought "it's probably not going to happen" and applied for the job search visa after seeing a video about it online. A few months after applying, my citizenship was approved. When the appointment invitation arrived a year later, I had already moved through another route; I didn't click the link, and I didn't attend the appointment.

In short, that application was my Plan B. If the citizenship hadn't come through, most of the waiting period would have already passed.

## 3 things you can do today

### 1. Start the application process

The current route for looking for a job in Germany is the **Chancenkarte** (Opportunity Card). It grants a residence permit of up to one year to come to Germany and look for work without having a job offer in hand beforehand. According to official information, the main requirements are roughly as follows:

- A university degree or vocational qualification fully recognized in Germany, **or** a degree/vocational qualification recognized in your home country + at least 6 points in the points system (based on criteria such as recognition, experience, German, English, age, and spouse).
- For the second path, at least A1 German or B2 English.
- Proof of financial self-sufficiency: at least €1,091 per month in a blocked account (Sperrkonto, 2026 figure).
- You can work part-time up to 20 hours a week while looking for a job.

The application is submitted via the German Federal Foreign Office's online portal (Auslandsportal). In Turkey, national visa appointments are scheduled through iDATA. Requirements can change; make sure to check the official resources below before applying.

### 2. Change your location on LinkedIn

This is something most people overlook, but it is crucial. I have also recruited candidates through LinkedIn. When creating a job posting, if you choose a filter like "do not accept applications from outside Turkey," candidates outside that location are automatically filtered out; their profile doesn't even appear in front of the recruiter. Similarly, when a company in Germany specifies "applications from Germany only," it will never see a candidate whose location is set to Turkey.

So if you are applying for positions in Germany, **set your location to Germany** and select the city you want to live in.

If you don't know which city to pick, you can do what I did: I chose Berlin first. I followed job listings and browsed through them for a few months. Then I noticed that the highest number of opportunities in my field was in Frankfurt. After that, I changed my location to Frankfurt.

Industries also vary by region: Frankfurt is heavily focused on finance, Hamburg on port and logistics, and Stuttgart and Munich on automotive. You can make a choice by checking where your industry is concentrated.

### 3. Learn German while waiting

One year is plenty of time to start German from scratch and reach a solid foundation. It both boosts your Chancenkarte points and helps tremendously in everyday life and job interviews once you arrive here.

We also built **[Memorlex](https://memorlex.com/)** for studying German vocabulary; you can make use of it. I also shared my advice on language learning in my post [knowing a language vs. being able to speak it](/en/career/knowing-a-language-vs-speaking-it/).

## In short

The process is long and not easy. You can't control how long the wait is, but you can control when it begins. Submit your application today, update your LinkedIn location, and start learning German.
