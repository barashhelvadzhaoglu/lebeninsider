Before I moved to Germany, I applied for the German **job seeker visa**. The invitation for the visa appointment arrived about a year later. By then my Bulgarian citizenship had come through, so as an EU citizen I no longer needed a visa and never continued the application.

So I can't tell you how the job seeker visa ends. What I can tell you is what the waiting taught me, and how the system works today, because the visa I applied for no longer exists.

## The job seeker visa is now the Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)

Since June 2024, Germany's job seeker visa has been replaced by the **Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)**. It is a residence permit for people from outside the EU who want to come to Germany to look for work.

There are two ways to qualify:

1. **Your qualification is fully recognised in Germany** (or you studied or trained in Germany). Then you count as a skilled worker and don't need points.
2. **Points system.** You need a completed vocational training (at least two years) or a university degree recognised in the country where you got it, **German at A1 or English at B2**, and **at least 6 points.**

Points are given for:

- Partial recognition of your qualification in Germany: 4 points
- Work experience: 2 points for 2 years in the last 5, 3 points for 5 years in the last 7
- German: A2 = 1, B1 = 2, B2 or higher = 3 points
- English at C1 or native level: 1 point
- Age: 2 points up to 35, 1 point from 35 to 40
- At least 6 months of legal stay in Germany in the last 5 years (not tourism): 1 point
- A qualification in a shortage occupation: 1 point
- A spouse or partner who also qualifies: 1 point

You must also show you can support yourself: in 2026 that means **at least €1,091 net per month**, usually through a blocked account (Sperrkonto).

The card is valid for up to **one year**. During that time you can work part-time up to **20 hours a week** and do trial work of up to two weeks per employer. If you find a suitable job, you switch to a work permit such as the EU Blue Card.

Always check the current rules on the official Make it in Germany website before you apply; amounts and details change.

## What the waiting taught me

**1. Start today, not when you feel ready.** The longest part was not preparing documents, it was waiting for an appointment. If I had waited until I felt ready, I would have lost another year. I wrote about this in more detail in [the first thing you should do if you want to move to Germany](/tr/almanya/expat/yurtdisina-tasinmak-istiyorsan-ilk-adim/) (in Turkish).

**2. Look at every route in parallel.** I applied for the visa while also working on my Bulgarian citizenship. The second route finished first. If you have any other option, such as EU citizenship through family, a job offer, or studying, follow it at the same time.

**3. Prepare your documents early.** Diplomas, translations and proof of funds all take time. Having them ready means you can act the moment an appointment or an offer appears.

**4. Learn some German while you wait.** It gives you points for the Opportunity Card and, more importantly, makes job interviews and daily life in Germany much easier.

## In short

I applied for the job seeker visa, waited about a year for an appointment, and then didn't need it. Today that visa is the Opportunity Card, with a points system and a blocked account of at least €1,091 a month in 2026. My main advice: start now, and follow every route you have at the same time.
