When we moved from Turkey to Germany, we drove our 2020 Honda Civic, which runs on LPG. The plan was simple: drive the whole way on cheap LPG. That worked perfectly through Bulgaria and Romania. Then we reached Budapest, and our LPG journey ended there.

This post explains what happened and what anyone driving an LPG car from Turkey to Europe should know before setting off.

## The problem: LPG nozzles are not the same everywhere

Most LPG cars in Turkey have a **DISH** filler connector. Pumps in Turkey, Bulgaria and Romania fit it. But Europe does not use a single standard. In Germany and Austria the common type is **ACME**, and some countries use **Bayonet**.

So even when you find a station with an "Autogas" sign, the pump nozzle may not fit your car. The fix is a small, cheap part: an **LPG adapter** that connects the pump nozzle to your filler.

## What happened to us

We left Istanbul with a full tank. We filled up with LPG several times in Bulgaria and Romania without any problems, and prices were good.

In Budapest we tried several stations. We saw the "Autogas" signs, but the nozzle did not fit our car. The attendants asked if we had an adapter; we didn't. One station tried an adapter they had, and it did not fit our connector either.

From there on, we drove through Austria and Germany, the last **800–900 kilometres, on petrol**. In Germany we saw LPG pumps at the big motorway service areas but could not use them. It was the most frustrating part of the trip, all because of a part that costs a few euros.

## Before you leave

1. **Buy an adapter set in Turkey.** Sets that convert DISH to ACME and Bayonet are sold by LPG installers, car parts shops and online. Finding one that fits a Turkish car on the road, especially in Western Europe, is very hard.
2. **Test the adapter at home.** Make sure it fits your filler before you are standing at a pump abroad.
3. **Mark LPG stations on your route in advance.** There are websites and apps that list LPG stations, and searching "LPG" or "Autogas" in Google Maps also helps. In Germany, LPG is not available at every station; it is mostly at certain motorway service areas and around cities.
4. **Fill up in the last DISH-compatible country.** Fill the LPG tank completely before leaving Bulgaria and Romania.
5. **Never let the petrol tank run low.** If you cannot find LPG or the adapter does not fit, you need to be able to continue on petrol.
6. **Carry some cash in euros.** Foreign cards occasionally cause problems at smaller stations.

## Is LPG still worth it in Germany?

LPG is cheaper than petrol in Germany, but the difference is much smaller than in Turkey or Eastern Europe. On the drive, the real savings happen in Bulgaria and Romania. Inside Germany, the hassle of finding a station eats into the advantage. Prices change often, so check a station app before you go.

## What if the car stays in Germany?

We eventually sold our Civic in Turkey. Once you live in Germany, keeping a Turkish-registered car for a long time is not practical; there are registration, insurance and customs rules. If you want to keep your car in Germany, check the rules for your situation on the German customs (Zoll) website and with an expert before you move.

## In short

Driving an LPG car from Turkey to Germany is possible and saves real money on the first half of the trip. But **do not leave without an adapter set.** The reason we drove the last 800–900 km on petrol was a part that costs a few euros.
