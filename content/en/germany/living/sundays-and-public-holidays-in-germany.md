One of the things you have to get used to after moving from Turkey: life in Germany works quite differently on Sundays. It is the same on public holidays.

## Supermarkets are closed on Sundays

Supermarkets like Kaufland are closed on Sundays. Opening on a Sunday requires a special permit. Restaurants, takeaway places, and ice cream parlors are generally open, though. On Sundays, ice cream shops get extremely busy with long queues.

That is why if we are planning a Sunday outing, we do our shopping on Saturday. You can check out my post on [preparing for a day trip with kids](/tr/almanya/seyahat/cocuklarla-gunubirlik-gezi-hazirligi/) regarding this.

## Kiosks and petrol stations

When everywhere is closed, you can pick up a few things from kiosks or petrol stations. People also use the cafés inside petrol stations to meet up with friends.

## Holidays and events throughout the year

There is always something happening here throughout the year: Oktoberfest was just on, then Halloween comes, followed by Christmas markets, then Easter... Some of these are events rather than public holidays. But alongside annual leave, there are also plenty of official public holidays.

Just for reference: nationwide public holidays in Germany include New Year's Day, Easter (Karfreitag and Ostermontag), May 1st, Himmelfahrt, Pfingstmontag, October 3rd, and Christmas (December 25–26). Holidays vary by federal state; in Bavaria, days like Heilige Drei Könige (January 6), Fronleichnam, and in some regions August 15 are added to these. Occasionally, a public holiday pops up in the summer months as well.

## Bridge days

If a holiday falls on a Thursday, the vast majority of people take Friday off as well, turning it into a four-day weekend. Since Himmelfahrt and Fronleichnam always fall on Thursdays, they are the most well-known examples of this.

In my opinion, this is a great setup for employees.

## Holidays falling on a Saturday

If a public holiday falls on a Saturday, supermarkets are closed on that Saturday too. This means that sometimes everywhere is closed for two consecutive days, Saturday and Sunday; you really have to plan your shopping in advance.
