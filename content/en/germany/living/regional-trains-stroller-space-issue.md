We usually travel as a family on regional trains. Let me tell you about their good and annoying sides.

## I find regional trains very good

Especially on trains coming from the Augsburg direction, almost every seat has a power outlet, on almost every train. Many people who work in Munich and live in other cities meet their electricity needs on the train; they charge their laptops and phones on the way.

There are tables, a luggage rack above your seat, and a hanger by the window. Even a long journey passes comfortably.

## Long journey with Deutschlandticket

On one trip, we met a family. They had boarded a train from Salzburg with the Deutschlandticket (a monthly public transport ticket valid across Germany), arrived in Munich, and were going to a place near Stuttgart from there. They had set off in the morning; with transfers, you can travel from Salzburg to Stuttgart or near Frankfurt in 7–8 hours, using only regional trains.

It's not fast, but it's possible to cover this distance with a 63-euro monthly ticket. I also explained the Deutschlandticket in my [transportation post](/en/germany/living/cars-traffic-turkey-vs-germany/).

## Stroller space: our biggest problem

When we go to Pasing or other places by bus from near our home, the buses are usually empty. There's a designated area for strollers on buses. But people, even if the bus is empty, even if they're getting off in two or three stops, young or old, always stand in this area or sit on the fold-down seats there. We end up stuck in the middle of the aisle with our stroller. Frankly, this really annoys us.

The same thing happens on the train; people sit on the fold-down seats. Once, a ticket inspector came and said, "Why are you standing in the middle? This is an area for bicycles and strollers." When I said, "I can't ask anyone to move," the inspector asked the people to move, and we were able to place the stroller.

I still can't understand this behavior. Even if the bus or train is empty, people don't move to the back or the middle.

## A small tip

If you have time, board the train at its first departure station. Instead of Pasing, we sometimes go to Hauptbahnhof (main station); it's much easier to find both a seat and space for a stroller where the train first departs.
