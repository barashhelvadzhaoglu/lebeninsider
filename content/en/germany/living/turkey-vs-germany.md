For someone born and raised in Turkey, Germany is a completely different world. Not just the language: habits, rules, the rhythm of daily life, all of it is different.

When people move to a new country or city, they unconsciously look for their old habits. The corner shop, getting things done quickly, the routine they are used to. When they cannot find them, everything feels strange and different.

## Not better or worse, just different

I lived through this myself, and over time I understood something: judging these differences as "good" or "bad" is a mistake. You are looking at a life built on a completely different culture and a completely different way of living. What is easy in one place is hard in the other, and what is a struggle in one is comfortable in the other.

So please do not read this series as a "which is better" contest. You will see that I find Germany easier in some areas and Turkey easier in others.

## Decide first

If you are thinking of moving from Turkey to Germany, from Germany to Turkey, or anywhere else, you need to make that decision first. Then read comparisons like these with one question in mind: what will I find when I get there, and what are my expectations?

## Rank your criteria

The real question is: which criteria matter when you make this decision, and how much does each one weigh for you? Everyone's priorities are different. For one person it is their children's education, for another law and safety, for someone else the economy.

Write down your own criteria and know how much each one matters to you. A country that is great by one criterion can be hard for you by another.

## What is in this series?

In this series I go through my own criteria and what I have experienced first-hand:

- The criteria behind my decision to move
- Education and the Ausbildung system
- Raising children: space, safety, independence
- The health system
- Working life and the social system
- Trust and rules in society
- Housing and living space
- Getting around

My priorities may not be yours. What matters is that you find your own as you read.
