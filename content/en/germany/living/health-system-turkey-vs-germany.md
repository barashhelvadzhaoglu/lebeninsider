*This post is part of the "Turkey or Germany?" series. It is not a contest of better or worse; it is about two different cultures and what to expect. Series intro: [Before you start comparing](/en/germany/living/turkey-vs-germany/)*

This post shows the "not better or worse, just different" idea of this series best, because in some health matters I find Turkey easier.

## Turkey: fast service

The health system in Turkey is much easier. Services in general work much better there. Whether you go to a public hospital or, with supplementary insurance, to a private one, you really get seen quickly.

## Germany: first, find a doctor

In Germany this is harder. When we moved here, we could not find a doctor for ourselves or for the children. Your child is sick and has a fever, you call a doctor, and they say: "If you are not registered with us, we cannot see you." Even when we said it was urgent, they did not accept. You have to go to the doctor you are registered with.

When you do see a doctor, you don't get the kind of attention you are used to in Turkey. Maybe this is cultural, I am not sure. As far as I understand, people here are expected to know their own bodies. In Turkey we go to the doctor for a sore throat or a cough; here you do not see a doctor for simple things like that. I had already seen this when I lived in the UK: supermarkets sold strong 3-in-1 or 5-in-1 cold remedies.

Maybe doctors are not paid enough by the insurers, I don't know. But sometimes you really have to insist to get an appointment.

## Some different practices

- When you go to the practice with a sick child, you wait in a separate room; sick and healthy patients wait apart. Sometimes they even tell you not to come in.
- Sometimes they tell you on the phone that you don't need to come. Your registered doctor sends the prescription to your health insurance card, you go to the pharmacy, hand over the card and pick up the medicine. Getting a prescription without seeing a doctor does not exist in Turkey. It is good in one way and bad in another.

## Advantages: sick days and leave

- If you don't feel well, you can stay home, usually for up to three days without a doctor's note.
- Annual leave is long. The legal minimum is 20 working days, and many companies give 25–30 days; 30 days is about six weeks.

In Turkey we usually had two weeks of leave. Your child gets sick, you have errands at the bank or a government office, so you take half days here and there. By the end of the year you were left with maybe one week you could actually use.
