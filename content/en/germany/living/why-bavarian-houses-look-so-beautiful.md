When wandering through Bavarian towns like Mittenwald, the first thing that catches your eye is the houses. Homes with painted, frescoed facades (known as *Lüftlmalerei*). In fact, we combined three symbols in the LebenInsider logo; one of them is German houses, and another is beer.

It is no coincidence that these houses always look so neat and charming.

## You can't just build however you want

When building or renovating a house here, you can't just do whatever you feel like; you need permission. If the house has historic preservation status (*Denkmalschutz*), you are required to preserve its original character. You will see this noted in property listings for sale as well.

At one point, I looked into buying a plot of land and building a house. What I found was this: makeshift, haphazard construction simply isn't permitted. You have to build while adhering to strict rules regarding exterior appearance, heat loss, and energy efficiency ratings. These are determined in the local zoning development plan (*Bebauungsplan*) and the municipality's design guidelines; on the energy side, there is the Building Energy Act (*GEG* - Gebäudeenergiegesetz).

At first, I thought building a steel-frame house was completely forbidden. Actually, it is generally not forbidden; the rules are mostly about what the house looks like from the outside.

## The result: a preserved historic look

As a result of these rules, you don't see random, eyesore houses popping up around you; the towns constantly preserve their historic atmosphere. For people visiting these towns, this holds a very special charm. In my opinion, it is also a fantastic way to attract tourists.
