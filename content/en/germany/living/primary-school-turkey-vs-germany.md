*This post is part of the "Turkey or Germany?" series. It is not a contest of better or worse; it is about two different cultures and what to expect. Series intro: [Before you start comparing](/en/germany/living/turkey-vs-germany/)*

My children's future was the most important criterion in my decision to move, and education is at the heart of it.

## The Turkish side

In Turkey, nurseries and private schools are very expensive; an ordinary employee can end up spending half of their salary on them. Everyone gets a university degree, yet the country is full of unemployed graduates.

Yes, your children can be very bright in Turkey and get into very good universities. But scandals like the diploma scandal we went through did great damage to both individuals and institutions. I see that as a big disadvantage.

The content of education, the quality of universities and the issue of "universities set up in apartment buildings" are separate topics that deserve their own post.

## The German side: Ausbildung

Germany has many different paths. One of them is **Ausbildung**, vocational training: you work at a company and attend vocational school for that job at the same time. Now that I live here, I see this first-hand. A few examples:

- A young man I met at the gym: he works as a trainer in a fitness centre and is doing an Ausbildung in that profession at the same time. The training also covers the marketing and sales side of the business.
- A woman doing an Ausbildung in IT purchasing.
- Another woman doing an Ausbildung in hotel purchasing.

So people can build a profession by working, without a university degree.

## My observation

From this point of view I think the education system here, or let us call it the principle of equal opportunity in education, offers more advantages. A child does not have only one road; university is an option, and so is Ausbildung.

Of course, this is my assessment for my own children. Which matters more for you depends on your own criteria.
