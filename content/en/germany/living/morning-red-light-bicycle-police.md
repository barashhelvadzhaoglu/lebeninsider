This morning around 6:30 AM, I was cycling to work. I wasn't fully awake yet. At an intersection, the light had turned red, but I hadn't noticed. By the time I did, it was already too late; instead of hitting the brakes, I sped up a bit and crossed. An old car was coming from the side street; the driver saw me and waited. They probably understood the situation.

About 200–300 meters later, someone caught up behind me and started saying something in German. Since my German isn't great, I didn't quite catch it at first. In the end, he said something like "stop here." I stopped and turned my head: a bicycle cop. He was patrolling on his bike at that hour, checking commuters—or maybe he was just heading to work himself.

"You ran a red light," he said. "You're right, I was zoned out, sorry about that," I replied. Then he pointed to the phone on my handlebars: "Why is the phone there?" I explained that I deliver for Lieferando, which is why the phone has to be mounted on the handlebars. Since I was going to work my delivery shift in the evening anyway, my Lieferando jacket and delivery bag were already attached to the bike.

"Actually, that's two violations," he said, "50 euros each, so I should write a 100 euro ticket." Struggling to express myself clearly, I told him I was unfocused and couldn't stop in time. He probably noticed how sleepy I looked. Somewhat annoyed, he said, "go on, get going." I thanked him, and he didn't even respond. I rode off, glancing back repeatedly; luckily, he didn't follow me.

I had experienced something similar once before. My earphones were in my pocket, I didn't feel like fishing them out, so I held my phone in my hand and rode slowly along the edge of the street. A police car drove past me and announced directly over the loudspeaker: put the phone away.

## What did I learn from this?

In Turkey, bicycles are often treated as something "outside" of traffic. That is not the case in Germany. A bicycle is considered a vehicle, and traffic rules are enforced just as strictly on cyclists. And the police don't just patrol in cars; they ride bikes too and actively monitor traffic.

When I looked it up later in the official fines catalog (Bußgeldkatalog), here are the actual figures:

- **Running a red light (on a bicycle):** If the light has been red for less than 1 second, it's €60; if longer than 1 second, €100. In both cases, you get 1 penalty point in Flensburg (the federal driving-record registry). If you endanger anyone, the fine goes up.
- **Using a phone while cycling:** €55. Simply holding the phone in your hand counts as an offense.
- **Phone on the handlebars:** Using a phone mounted in a holder for navigation is generally fine, as long as you don't actively operate it while riding. So having the phone mounted in a holder isn't a violation by itself; interacting with it while riding is.

I got lucky this time. But I learned loud and clear that cycling early in the morning with a groggy head carries both real penalties and real risks.

*Note: Fine amounts may change over time. For up-to-date information, check resources like [bussgeldkatalog.de](https://www.bussgeldkatalog.de/rote-ampel-fahrrad/).*
