*This post is part of the "Turkey or Germany?" series. It is not a contest of better or worse; it is about two different cultures and what to expect. Series intro: [Before you start comparing](/en/germany/living/turkey-vs-germany/)*

Giving my children a better future was my number one criterion. After moving here I understood better that this is not only about education.

## One park in Istanbul, 50 children

When I lived in Istanbul there was a park in front of my home, actually two parks next to each other. When we went out at the weekend, or when I went for an evening walk, there were, without exaggeration, more than 50 children in that park. Little space, lots of children; everyone squeezed into the same place.

## A playground in front of every building in Germany

In Germany, almost every building and apartment block has a small park or green area in front of it. Many have playgrounds and sandpits. Children can grow up close to nature, with much more space around them.

Children leave their toys in the sandpit and go home, and nobody takes them.

## Children who grow up confident

Another point: children here grow up with self-confidence. Many children cycle to school on their own. Five- or six-year-olds take their school bag and walk to school by themselves.

Unfortunately, we cannot let them do that in Turkey.

## What it means to me

When I think about my children's future, these things matter at least as much as education: growing up closer to nature, being able to do things on their own, feeling safe.

Of course this space comes at a price; everyday needs like the supermarket are further away. I cover that in the housing and transport posts of this series.
