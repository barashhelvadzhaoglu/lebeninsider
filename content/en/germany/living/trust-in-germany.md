I think this is one of the biggest differences between Turkey and Germany: trust in society and the attitude towards rules.

## The public enforces the rules

Here people follow the rules themselves. If you park your bike or your car in the wrong place, or an adult plays in a children's playground, the older residents act almost like police; they come straight over and tell you: "This is wrong, you can't do that, you can't park here."

When we first moved here, I rented a car and parked it in the wrong spot on the side street in front of our building. Later a neighbour told me: the older residents had called the police, and the police had come and given the car a ticket. The fine was later charged to me through the rental company.

So there are rules, and the ones enforcing them are not only the police but the public itself. In Turkey we park somewhere and say "five minutes won't hurt," we block the road, we do things without following the rules and say "what's the big deal." That doesn't happen here; people step in immediately.

## Small details built on trust

The other side of this is trust:

- **Toys left in the sandpit:** Children leave their toys in the sandpit in front of the building and nobody takes them.
- **Backpacks in the supermarket:** A scene you often see on social media too: people put their shopping into their backpacks while they shop, take it out at the checkout to scan it, then put it back. Nobody checks whether something was left in the bag.
- **Things left for others:** People leave things they no longer need outside their door, and whoever needs them takes them. Nobody asks "who took it, Ahmet or Mehmet?" Nobody has to deal with anyone, and nobody questions whether that person really needs it.

## Feeling safe

In a society like this you feel safer. In my opinion, this is the biggest difference compared to Turkey.

The other side of the coin is that people step in even over small rule breaks, and getting used to that can take time.
