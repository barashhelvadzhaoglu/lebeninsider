In my opinion, one of the best parts of city life in Germany is the **Kleingarten** (allotment garden). These are areas right within the city where small gardens are lined up side by side.

## What is it?

It's a small garden within the city containing one or two small hut-style structures. You don't own the land; you rent it from the municipality or a local association (Verein). You buy the hut itself from the previous tenant. From what I've heard, this can cost around 5,000–6,000 to 10,000 euros.

## What can you do?

- You cultivate the garden: you can grow vegetables, fruits, and fruit trees.
- You can barbecue.
- Kids can run around and play freely in the garden.

You cannot stay overnight. There is usually well water, but no electricity; people try to solve this by installing solar panels.

## Our experience

Some of our acquaintances have a Kleingarten, and we visit them sometimes. You can spend time there from morning till evening on weekends. If the weather is nice, you can go there for an hour or two after work on weekdays for tea, coffee, or maybe dinner, and then head home.

It's great for relaxing. You don't need to live outside the city to escape city life.

## Hard to find

There is huge demand. I applied once; they weren't even taking anyone onto the waiting lists anymore. I also see listings on Kleinanzeigen, but I haven't looked into the details yet.

If you are considering a Kleingarten, start researching early and ask about the waiting lists of local associations in your area.
