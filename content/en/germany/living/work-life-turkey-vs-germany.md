*This post is part of the "Turkey or Germany?" series. It is not a contest of better or worse; it is about two different cultures and what to expect. Series intro: [Before you start comparing](/en/germany/living/turkey-vs-germany/)*

For someone coming from Turkey, working life and the social system in Germany are among the things that feel most different.

## Extra income with a mini job

Germany has so-called mini jobs, small side jobs that let you earn extra income. I deliver orders by bike for Lieferando; that is my mini job.

## Neither poor nor very rich

The system here works like this: they will not let you die of poverty, but they will not let you become very rich either.

As far as I understand, the state does a calculation: your rent, plus roughly 500–550 euros per adult and roughly 300–350 euros per child, and says "this is the minimum you should have." If you earn below that, you receive social benefits. If you earn above it, there is no support, and if you earn a lot, a lot is taken in tax. (The amounts change every year; check official sources for exact figures.)

## Firing someone is hard

When you work for a company here on an employment contract, it is very hard to fire you. I sometimes hear that people who sense they are about to be let go get a sick note and stay off work for months, and the company cannot easily dismiss them.

## Laws that protect tenants

Similarly, there are laws that protect tenants very strongly. This is another point that feels very different compared to Turkey.

## What I think

This system is very comfortable for someone who wants steady security. But for someone who wants to get rich quickly, earn a lot and save a lot, high taxes can feel limiting. Which brings us back to the same question: what is your priority?
