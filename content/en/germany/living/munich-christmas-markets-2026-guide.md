At the end of November, Munich's atmosphere completely transforms: wooden huts are set up in the squares, the scent of Glühwein (mulled wine), roasted almonds, and grilled sausages fills the air, and everywhere glows brightly in the evenings. Christmas markets (Christkindlmarkt or Weihnachtsmarkt) are Germany's most beloved winter tradition.

In this post, I’ve gathered the 2026 dates and practical information. I will also be adding our own experiences and photos from the markets we visit this year.

## 1. Munich Christkindlmarkt (Marienplatz)

The city's best-known and largest Christmas market, set right in front of the New Town Hall (Neues Rathaus).

- **Dates:** November 20 – December 24, 2026. It opens a bit earlier than usual this year. **Closed on November 22 (Totensonntag - Sunday of the Dead).**
- **Hours:** Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00, Sunday 10:00–20:00, December 24 10:00–14:00.
- **Size:** Over 130 stalls across Marienplatz and Rindermarkt. In front of St. Peter's Church (Alter Peter), there is also the Kripperlmarkt, where nativity figurines are sold.
- **Transport:** Marienplatz via U3/U6 and all S-Bahn lines. There is no parking nearby, and bikes are not allowed inside the market area.

**For kids:**

- **Himmelswerkstatt:** A free craft workshop inside the Town Hall for children aged 6–12. Daily from November 26 to December 13, 14:30–16:00 and 16:30–18:00. Registration is required (via Kindermuseum München, opens 10 days before the requested date).
- **Krampuslauf:** Sunday, December 13, 15:00–17:00, Krampus run in the old town. The costumes can be quite scary, so give younger kids a heads-up beforehand.
- **Singen unterm Christbaum:** Free communal Christmas carol singing in front of Michaelskirche (November 25, December 2, 9, and 16 at 16:30).

## 2. Tollwood Winter Festival (Theresienwiese)

A more "alternative" Christmas market set up on the Oktoberfest grounds.

- **Dates:** November 24 – December 23, 2026.
- **Hours:** Opens at 14:00 on weekdays and 11:00 on weekends; closes at 22:30 on Mondays and Sundays, and at 23:30 Tuesday through Saturday.
- **Admission:** Free. Most events are free; only the performances in the grand tent require tickets.
- **What’s there:** Handcrafted goods, jewelry, and gifts across about 200 stalls; more than 50 organic food stalls; live music and street performances. Great for those wanting to try different things, as it offers dishes from world cuisines.
- **Transport:** Theresienwiese via U4/U5.

## 3. Other Markets

Every neighborhood has its own little market. Their 2026 dates haven't been fully announced yet, but they are generally open from the last week of November until Christmas:

- **Medieval Market (Mittelaltermarkt), Wittelsbacherplatz:** Vendors in medieval attire, artisan crafts, food cooked over open wood fires. Very interesting for kids.
- **Weihnachtsdorf, Residenz:** A fairytale Christmas village nestled in the palace courtyard.
- **Chinesischer Turm, Englischer Garten:** Surrounded by trees inside the park; featuring Bavarian curling (Eisstock) lanes right next to it.
- **Pink Christmas, Stephansplatz:** A colorful and vibrant market.
- **Haidhausen (Weißenburger Platz) and Sendlinger Tor:** Smaller, calmer markets with a genuine neighborhood feel.

Before heading out, be sure to check dates and hours on the markets' official websites or on muenchen.de.

## Glühwein and Mug Deposit (Pfand)

At Christmas markets, drinks are usually served in ceramic mugs, and a **mug deposit (Pfand)** is added on top of the price. When you return the mug, you get your deposit back; alternatively, you can keep the mug as a souvenir. Every market—and sometimes even every year—has a different mug design.

There are non-alcoholic options too: **Kinderpunsch** (hot fruit punch) for kids, and hot chocolate.

## What to Eat?

- Roasted almonds (gebrannte Mandeln)
- Grilled sausage in a roll (Bratwurst)
- Potato pancakes (Reiberdatschi / Kartoffelpuffer) with applesauce
- Gingerbread cookies (Lebkuchen)
- Stollen (traditional fruit cake)

Prices can be higher compared to a regular café. I explained why eating out is generally expensive in Germany in this post.

## Tips for Going with Kids

- **Go on weekdays and during the day.** Marienplatz gets very crowded in the evenings and on weekends; navigating with a stroller becomes difficult.
- **Consider a baby carrier instead of a stroller**, especially for Marienplatz.
- **Dress warmly.** You’ll be walking outside in the cold for hours; dressing in layers, gloves, and a beanie are essential.
- **Carry cash.** Many stalls accept cards, but not all of them.
- **Try the smaller markets.** Neighborhood markets are quieter and much more relaxed with kids.

Before the Christmas markets, there is another tradition in November: the [St. Martin lantern procession](/en/germany/living/st-martin-lantern-procession-germany/).
