*This post is part of the "Turkey or Germany?" series. It is not a contest of better or worse; it is about two different cultures and what to expect. Series intro: [Before you start comparing](/en/germany/living/turkey-vs-germany/)*

In the series intro I said that everyone should rank their own criteria before changing countries. Here are mine.

## 1. A better future for my children

For me, the top priority was being able to give my children a better future. I believe I can do that better in Germany.

In Turkey, nurseries and private schools are very expensive. An ordinary employee may have to spend half of their salary on them. And there is another issue: everyone gets a university degree, and the country is full of unemployed graduates. That is not what I want to offer my child in the future. (I cover education separately in the next post of this series.)

## 2. Law, rights and justice

Another criterion was law, rights and justice. In Turkey, the way laws are applied is problematic; we see and witness different treatment and different rules depending on the person or the institution. That was one of the things that made me feel unsafe.

## 3. Inflation

The other issue was high inflation. Turkey has gone through very hard times in recent years, and I lived through part of it myself.

I remember this clearly: when my son was 2, I was buying diapers for 50 kuruş each and filling my car with LPG at 2–2.5 lira per litre. About three years later, when my son turned 5, the fuel I put in my car cost 27–28 lira, almost ten times more. Diapers went up to 3–4 lira each.

But my salary did not go up tenfold. I worked for good companies in Turkey, I work in IT, and I earned what you would call an above-average salary. Even so, the balance did not hold, and it got worse every year.

## Your criteria may be different

These are my criteria. For a single person, a retiree or someone starting their own business, the priorities may be completely different. What matters is knowing how much weight you give to each criterion.
