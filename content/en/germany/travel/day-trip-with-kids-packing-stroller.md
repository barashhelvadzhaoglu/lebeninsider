On weekends, we usually go on day trips with our two young sons using regional trains. Over time, we have developed a routine. Here, I share what we pack under the stroller and what we pay attention to.

## Water and formula

- **Water:** At least 2–3 1.5-liter bottles of water.
- **Water bottle:** We usually take just one kid's water bottle. My younger son does whatever his older brother does; they manage with a single water bottle.
- **Formula:** Hot water in a thermos, along with about two spoonfuls of powdered formula. When needed, we prepare it right there.

## Fruit and bakery items

We usually take two kinds of fruit, like bananas and apples.

If the trip is on a Sunday, we shop on Saturday from the bakery section of supermarkets: croissants, various breads with whole wheat and seeds, Brezel (pretzels), donut-style pastries. Every supermarket has its own specialties. On the sweet side, there is plenty of apple pie and cake, as well as cherry tarts. That’s how the space under the stroller fills up.

## Bringing cooked food is difficult

Since the kids are small, things like sandwiches, cheese, olives, and tomatoes are not very practical. We rarely bring cooked food: things the kids love like bulgur pilaf, rice, or pasta. But carrying it is troublesome; when the weather is warm, there is a risk of it spoiling. We haven't tried it in winter yet.

A friend gave us a recommendation: wide-mouth camping food thermoses. They bought 8–10 of them; they put soup in two, cooked meals in two, and open and eat them in the park. Since it's tough spending time at home with two young boys, they spend their days outside like this too. We'll try it soon as well.

## Why so much preparation?

First, because we travel on regional trains, the journey is slow and long. We pass the time by eating and snacking on the train. If the train is very crowded, it gets tough; once it empties out after the central stations, we can relax.

Second, eating out is expensive; I will write about that in a separate post.

If what we brought isn't enough, we shop at supermarkets in the town we visit. Wherever we go, we usually visit museums, churches, historic buildings, and public squares. When the kids see french fries or ice cream, they want some; so we buy those on the spot.

## Watch out on Sundays

In Germany, supermarkets (Kaufland, etc.) are closed on Sundays; a special permit is required to open on a Sunday. Restaurants, takeaway spots, and ice cream parlors are generally open. On Sundays, ice cream shops get extremely busy with long lines.

When everything is closed, you can grab a few things from kiosks or gas stations.
