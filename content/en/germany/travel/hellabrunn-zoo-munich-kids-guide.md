Today we are heading to the zoo in Munich again, **Tierpark Hellabrunn**. It is a wonderful place to visit with kids, especially on weekends. In this post, I explain how to get there, ticket prices, the annual pass, and what you need to know as a family with children.

## Where is Hellabrunn, and what is it like?

Hellabrunn is located in the south of Munich, easily accessible from the city, and right **on the banks of the Isar River**. In that respect, its location is fantastic.

The area is huge: about **40 hectares**. It has been open since 1911 and is home to around **500 species** of animals. Giraffes, gorillas, elephants, lions, wolves, polar bears... You can view the animals comfortably and up close. Over two million people visit each year.

Because the park is large, walking distances are long as well; plan for about an hour of walking while exploring. Wear comfortable shoes.

## How to get there?

You can reach it by both subway (U-Bahn) and bus. There are **two entrance gates**, and you can use whichever you prefer:

- **Isar-Eingang (Isar entrance):** Take the **U3** subway line and get off at the **Thalkirchen (Tierpark)** stop; it's about a 3-minute walk to the gate.
- **Flamingo-Eingang (Flamingo entrance):** **Bus line 52** departs from Marienplatz, and its last stop "Tierpark (Alemannenstraße)" is right in front of the gate.

The zoo recommends taking the **U3** for visitors coming with strollers, as space for strollers on buses is limited. Until the end of November 2026, due to construction work on the U3 between Sendlinger Tor and Implerstraße, replacement buses are operating; keep this in mind if you are coming from the city center.

You can also go by car, but parking is limited and paid. On weekends, I recommend public transport. If you have a Deutschlandticket, transportation is already included.

For us, getting there is convenient: First, we take a bus for 10–15 minutes, then we board the subway at the end of the line for another 10–15 minutes. Once we exit the station, we walk straight into the zoo.

## Prices and the annual ticket

My favorite option here is the **annual ticket (Jahreskarte)**. You buy it once, and you can visit as many times as you want for a full year.

| Ticket | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Adult day ticket | €23 |
| Child day ticket (ages 4–14) | €9 |
| Small family day ticket (1 parent + kids) | €28 |
| Large family day ticket (2 parents + kids) | €51 |
| **Adult annual ticket** | **€69** |
| Child annual ticket (ages 4–14) | €34 |
| Small family annual ticket | €69 |
| Large family annual ticket | €138 |

You don't need to buy a separate ticket for **children under 4 years old**. Since our kids are small, we only bought annual tickets for ourselves.

The math is simple: An adult day ticket is €23, and the annual ticket is €69. So if you go **three times a year**, the annual ticket pays for itself.

And here is a nice detail: If your child turns 4 while your annual ticket is still valid, you can get a free child card from the service desk; it remains valid until the same expiration date as your pass.

Opening hours: In the winter season (October 25 – March 19) it is open daily from **09:00–17:00**, and in summer until 18:00. Open every day of the year.

## Ideal for Sundays

In Germany, supermarkets and most shops are closed on Sundays; I explained this in my post on [Sundays and public holidays](/en/germany/living/sundays-and-public-holidays-in-germany/). The zoo, however, remains open. That makes it an excellent activity, especially on Sundays.

Of course, since everyone thinks the same way, **it gets very crowded in the summer**. People visit heavily in winter too, but not as much as in summer.

## Strollers, scooters, and bicycles

- **Strollers:** You can enter without any problem; the paths are wide. Today, we're bringing either our wide double stroller or our bike trailer. When you attach the front wheels to the trailer, it can be used like a stroller, and it's also great for carrying gear.
- **Scooters and bicycles are forbidden:** Bicycles, scooters (*Roller*), skateboards, and e-scooters are not allowed inside. Once, we couldn't bring our child's scooter inside and had to leave it outside the entrance. Thank goodness nobody took it. But I wouldn't recommend doing that; leave scooters at home.
- **Laufrad (balance bikes without pedals) allowed:** Balance bikes without pedals are allowed for young children.
- **Pull-along wagon rental (Bollerwagen):** For families with kids, there are wheeled pull-along wagons (*Bollerwagen*); you can rent them at the ticket counter for **€7** per day. You leave a photo ID as a deposit, and reservations cannot be made. You must return the wagon to the gate where you picked it up at least 15 minutes before closing.

For advice on choosing a stroller, you can check out my post on [stroller preferences](/en/germany/living/baby-stroller-guide-germany/).

## Playgrounds and picnics

Besides walking around to see the animals, there are also **playgrounds** inside for kids: slides, teeter-totters, and adventure play parks. There is also a petting zoo section where children can touch the animals.

People love bringing sandwiches and eating outdoors, especially in the summer. It's really lovely. We're packing food with us today as well; we plan to explore a bit and then eat there. There are restaurants and snack stands inside, but bringing your own food is both cheaper and more enjoyable.

Remember: **Feeding the animals is strictly forbidden** (including items like chestnuts and acorns).

I explained what to pack in my post on [day trip preparation with kids](/en/germany/travel/day-trip-with-kids-packing-stroller/).

## In summary

- A large 40-hectare area on the banks of the Isar; you can easily spend the whole day with kids.
- Take the U3 to Thalkirchen or bus line 52 to the Flamingo entrance.
- Adult day ticket €23, **annual ticket €69**; children under 4 enter free.
- Strollers allowed, scooters and bikes forbidden; wagons €7/day.
- A great option on Sundays when everything else is closed, but crowded in summer.
- Bring your own food and enjoy a picnic in the open area.

*Prices and opening hours are valid as of October 2026; check hellabrunn.de before going.*
