Today we went all the way to the Austrian border. Our goal wasn't to tour a big city; we just wanted to see the mountains, the greenery, and enjoy a nice train ride.

## Morning: bike, trailer, stroller

In the morning, my older son had a speech therapy appointment. We went there with the kids by bike. I have an e-bike with a trailer attached to the back; I use it to drop off and pick up the kids from school, and we also use it for outings.

I locked up the bike and trailer in Pasing. My wife brought the stroller from home, and we met at Pasing.

## Pasing or Hauptbahnhof?

Pasing is closer to our home, so we usually board there. But some trains run only every two hours. If we have time, we go to the Hauptbahnhof (central station) and get on where the train originates; it's much more comfortable. The direction toward Augsburg in particular gets very crowded: if you board at Pasing, you can't find a seat or fit the stroller.

## Toward the mountains with the RB6

There is a regional train (RB6, Werdenfelsbahn) going from Pasing to Scharnitz via Garmisch-Partenkirchen and Mittenwald. Today there was maintenance work almost everywhere across the metro, S-Bahn, and trains; at one point, we got off the train and continued with a rail replacement bus (SEV, Schienenersatzverkehr).

The scenery along the way is beautiful: lush greenery, mountain slopes, small lakes, and little rivers. A peaceful journey.

## Scharnitz: just across the border

To be honest, there isn't much to do in Scharnitz. We actually came just to cross the border and take a train ride along the mountain slopes. There is a place resembling a restaurant across from the station; the kids ran around and played in its parking lot. There were 30–45 minutes until the next bus. We fed the kids the food we brought along, drank some water, played, and then boarded the replacement bus heading to Mittenwald.

> **Ticket note:** The Deutschlandticket is valid up to Mittenwald. Since the stretch between Mittenwald and Scharnitz is in Austria, a separate ticket is required for that part.

## Mittenwald

In Mittenwald, we got off at the station and tried to reach the nearby cable car (Karwendelbahn). It is said to be the second highest cable car in Germany after the Zugspitze, with the top station located over 2,200 meters up.

There was roadwork and excavation all over town, and roads were closed, so we navigated through alternative routes. Mittenwald's colorful painted houses are a story of their own; I'll talk about that separately.

## Hikers, cyclists, campers

What caught my attention in this region is how connected people are with nature. So many people are hiking or cycling around. On the trains heading in this direction, you run into people heading up into the mountains with camping gear. Cyclists take the train to these towns and start climbing the mountains from there.

In and around Garmisch-Partenkirchen, there are lots of winter sports shops. You can rent things like ski gear or winter jackets. This industry is very vibrant here: there is high demand for hotels and accommodation, and going up with the cable car and skiing down is extremely popular.

## Who is it suitable for?

If you're expecting a big city trip, Scharnitz is not for you. But if you want to leave Munich, ride through the mountains by train for a few hours, and enjoy a quiet day with the kids, it's a great route. Don't forget to bring food with you; I explained what we prepare for a day trip with kids in a [separate post](/en/germany/travel/day-trip-with-kids-packing-stroller/).
