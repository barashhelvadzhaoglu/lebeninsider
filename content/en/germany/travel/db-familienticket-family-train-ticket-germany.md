On weekends, we usually travel on regional trains with our two young sons using our Deutschlandticket (the nationwide public transit subscription). Traveling to another city on an ICE, however, is a whole different calculation with two adult tickets and separate seat reservations for everyone.

Deutsche Bahn introduced a family ticket this month: the **Familienticket**. It was tested last summer just for the school holidays; now it's permanent. Sales started on October 26, 2026, and travel is possible **from November 1, 2026 onwards**.

I immediately looked into the details. Here, I briefly explain what's included, who can use it, and what to keep in mind.

## Price: €99.99 round-trip

- **Round-trip:** €99.99
- **One-way:** €59.99
- The price is **per ticket, not per person.** So whether 3 people travel or 5, it's the exact same price.
- **Seat reservations included.** The reservation fee that we normally pay separately for each passenger is included in the ticket price.

If five people travel, it comes out to under 20 euros per person. For a round trip across Germany on an ICE, this is an unbeatable price.

## Who can use it?

- Up to **5 people**.
- A maximum of **2 adults** (aged 15 and over) and up to **4 children** (aged 14 and under).
- **At least one child** must actually travel.
- **No relation required.** You don't have to be parents; grandparents, aunts, uncles, or friends traveling together can use it too.

On standard tickets, children up to age 14 already travel for free on ICE/IC trains when accompanied by parents or grandparents (they just need to be added to the ticket). However, seat reservations for each child still cost extra: €5.50 per person and direction in 2nd class. For families like ours with young kids, the real saving with this ticket comes from the price of the two adults and guaranteed seats for everyone. If you want separate seats for little children, having reservations included is a big deal.

## Which trains is it valid on?

- On **ICE, IC, and EC** trains, **within Germany**.
- **2nd class only.** No upgrades to 1st class.
- International connections are not included.
- **No City-Ticket.** Local public transportation at the destination city is not included in the ticket.

### If a regional train is required

If you need to use a regional train (such as an RE or RB) for part of your journey, you pay an **additional €22 per direction**. In that case, a round-trip comes out to **€143.99**. The main leg of the journey must be on an ICE/IC.

## How to buy it

1. Go to **bahn.de** or open the **DB Navigator** app (it can also be purchased at DB ticket counters).
2. When searching for connections, **be sure to add the children** to the passenger count. If you don't enter children, the Familienticket option will not appear.
3. If the Familienticket fare shows up for an eligible connection, select it; seat reservations are included in the booking flow.

According to some sources, the ticket must be purchased **at least 2 days before the journey**. Don't leave it to the last minute.

## Things to keep in mind

- **Train-specific booking (Zugbindung):** The ticket is valid only on the specific trains you selected when booking. You cannot take a train at another time. For instance, if you chose the ICE at 09:00 on November 15 for the outbound leg and the ICE at 18:00 on November 17 for the return, the ticket is valid only on those two trains. On the return trip, you don't need to take the exact counterpart train; you choose your return train separately during the booking process, and it can be on a different day. Make sure to select both outbound and return journeys in the same search: if you book them separately, you'll pay 2 × €59.99 = €119.98. However, if your train is delayed and an arrival delay of at least 20 minutes is expected, passenger rights allow you to take another train. In that case, your seat reservation does not automatically transfer to the new train.
- **Cancellation:** You can cancel **up to one day before the first day of validity** for a €10 fee; the refund is issued as a **voucher (Gutschein)**, not cash. Tickets bought online can be canceled free of charge within **3 hours** of purchase (provided the journey hasn't started yet). Once the first travel day arrives, cancellations are no longer possible.
- **Availability:** According to DB, there is no fixed quota, but the ticket may not be available on heavily booked trains. It's smart to book early, especially around Christmas and during school holidays.

## The math: at what price does it pay off?

Let's look at two adults and two children, round-trip, with seat reservations for everyone:

| | Regular ticket (Sparpreis) | Familienticket |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Children | Free | Included |
| Reservations (4 people × 2 directions × €5.50) | €44 | Included |
| Adults (2 people × 2 directions) | 4 × ticket price | Included |
| **Total** | **€44 + 4 × ticket price** | **€99.99** |

In other words, if the one-way Sparpreis per adult is **above roughly 14 euros**, the Familienticket is cheaper. On long-distance routes like Munich to Berlin or Hamburg, Sparpreis fares are usually well above that threshold.

Stiftung Warentest ran comparisons across several routes: on pure ICE/IC routes, even when searched three months in advance and without seat reservations, round-trip Sparpreis fares ranged between **€117 and €429**—meaning the Familienticket was cheaper in every single scenario. On mixed routes that include regional trains, the price difference narrows; in one example checked a few months ahead, a regular ticket was almost the same price as the Familienticket.

## Real-life example: 4 people from Munich to Cologne

I wanted to see how this works in practice rather than just in theory. On DB Navigator, I searched for Friday, November 13, **München-Pasing → Köln Hbf**, 4 people, 2nd class, one-way. For the 17:36 ICE connection (transferring across the same platform at Frankfurt Airport, total travel time 4 hours 40 minutes), here were the offers:

| Ticket | Price (4 people, one-way) | Seat reservation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **DB Familienticket** | **€59.99** | Included (€0) |
| Super Sparpreis | €159.98 | Extra charge |
| Sparpreis | €177.98 | Extra charge |

![Familienticket, Super Sparpreis, and Sparpreis prices for Munich–Cologne on DB Navigator](img/travel/db-familienticket-fiyat-karsilastirma.webp)

That's a difference of **over 100 euros** for the exact same train and the exact same 4 passengers—and when you add seat reservations, the gap widens even more.

Two things stood out to me:

- **It doesn't show up on every train.** Earlier connections that same evening started at €239.98 and €260.38; the Familienticket price was only available on certain trains. You have to check times individually.
- **You can pick your own seats.** The reservation is added at €0; on both trains (ICE 512 and ICE 526 after the transfer), I selected 4 seats together in the family area (Familienbereich). If you don't pick them, seats are assigned automatically. The app also notes that **the seat reservation cannot be canceled separately**.

## When does it make sense, and when does it not?

**When it makes sense:**
- Long-distance round trips with two adults and children (e.g., Munich–Berlin, Munich–Hamburg).
- Visiting relatives during school holidays.
- When you want guaranteed adjacent seating for the entire family.

**When you should compare alternatives:**
- A short trip with one adult and a young child: an advance **Sparpreis** ticket might turn out cheaper.
- If you need flexibility: because the ticket has train-binding (Zugbindung), you cannot change your departure time on a whim.
- If most of the journey is on regional trains: traveling with the **Deutschlandticket** might be significantly cheaper.
- If you don't need seat reservations: since children ride for free anyway, buying just adult tickets could occasionally cost less.
- If a child falls ill at the last minute: the ticket explicitly requires at least one child to actually travel; adults cannot travel on this ticket on their own.

My recommendation: when searching for a connection, check both the Familienticket and regular Sparpreis fares, and choose whichever is cheaper.

## In summary

- Up to 5 people, round-trip on ICE/IC for **€99.99**, one-way €59.99.
- Maximum 2 adults + up to 4 children, minimum 1 child required, no relation required.
- Seat reservations included, 2nd class only, train-specific (Zugbindung).
- If regional trains are needed: +€22 per direction.
- Cancellation: up to one day before departure for a €10 fee, refunded as a voucher (Gutschein).
- Valid from November 1, 2026, and here to stay permanently.
