*This post is part of my "Before Choosing a Career" series on career choice and career planning. All parts of the series: [Career](/tr/kariyer/)*

You've built your profile, and there are a few professions that appeal to you. Now you need to evaluate these careers. I suggest looking at four criteria.

## 1. How in-demand will it be in the future?

This is the first thing everyone looks at, and rightfully so. What will become of this profession in the future, and what are your chances of finding a job in this field? There are forecasts and studies online about how much demand a profession will see in the future. You need to find and evaluate them, using them as one of your criteria.

## 2. How much money will you make?

The second criterion is salary. For this, you can usually look at current average salaries in that profession.

## 3. How do people in that profession live?

In my opinion, this is one of the most important points: **what does life generally look like for people doing this job?**

You need to think about it like this: Do these people work from 8 AM to 5 PM, or at different hours? Let me give a few examples:

- Waiters and chefs in restaurants might start their day at 12 PM and work until 8 PM.
- Desk workers generally work between 8 AM and 5 PM.
- Certain professions, like security guards, have to work shifts.

The hours you work also determine the rest of your life.

## 4. What does a day, a week, or a month look like?

Another topic is this: how do the days, weeks, and months go in that profession? For example, what does an IT professional's day look like, what about a week, what about a month? You need to focus a bit on these questions. (I'll answer this question with an example from my own profession in the next post of the series.)

## The order matters

Here is my take on this: People usually look at professions and choose them thinking, "it pays better, it's more in demand." In my opinion, this is the wrong approach.

First, you need to know what you want: What do you love, what do you enjoy doing, how do you want to spend your time? Demand and salary are important criteria, but they only make sense when you evaluate a profession through this lens—that is, through what it brings to your own life.
