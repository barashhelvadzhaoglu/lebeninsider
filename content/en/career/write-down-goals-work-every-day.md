Those who want to switch careers, recent graduates, university students... Let's say you've decided on the profession you want to pursue. From this point on, you need to establish a **working routine**.

Instead of hiding behind excuses like "I'm so busy right now, I have so much work, I'm too tired," you need to get things done, even if it's just a little bit every day.

## Always write down your goals

This is already a proven fact: definitely write down your goals. Grab a pen and paper, or use whatever note-taking method works for you; some people sit down and write in a notebook, while others use a notes app on their phone. Use whichever tool feels comfortable. But make sure you write down your goals.

## My method

For my part, here is what I do. My mind usually struggles with categorizing things by topic, so I frame them **by time**:

- What I will do this week
- What I will do this month
- What I will do within three months
- What I will do within six months
- What I will do within a year

Then I write down the things I want to do, even if they are very simple: I want to buy a car, I want to buy a house, I want to find a job. I place these under the relevant group.

Next, I **elaborate** on each goal: I want a car, but let it be like this and that. I want a house, but it should be like this. I want a job, but with these specific conditions.

And if possible, I open these notes at least once or twice a week and review them.

Speaking for myself: so far, I've managed to achieve pretty much every goal I wrote down in one way or another. The ones I haven't achieved yet are sitting on the sidelines; they are very big goals, but I'm taking the necessary steps for them as well. When you review and check your goals at regular intervals, you eventually reach them over time.

## Work a little every day, even just a bit

Let's get to the main point: doing some work toward your goal every day, or every week, even if it's just a little bit.

You work long hours and want to switch careers. In the evening, while riding the company shuttle, bus, or metrobus, you can open a Udemy course and read or listen to it. I did this a lot back in the day. I watched so many training videos on the metrobus heading to Zincirlikuyu in Istanbul. Sometimes you have to watch a video a second or third time, but at least you make productive use of the time spent in traffic.

I know people are genuinely busy. They take care of kids and family, and have all kinds of worries. But you need to work toward these goals at least one or two days a week, a few times.

## The power of repetition

Especially for subjects like learning a language, you really need to put in the work. When you first start studying, it won't click right away; you won't understand it. But when you try doing the same thing over and over, after the fifth repetition, after the tenth repetition, it will click.

In other words, you need to work toward your goals **without giving up**.
