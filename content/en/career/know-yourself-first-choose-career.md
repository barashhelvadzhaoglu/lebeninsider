*This post is the first part of the "Before Choosing a Career" series I prepared on career choice and career planning. All parts of the series: [Career](/tr/kariyer/)*

When choosing a career, most of us look at the same questions: How in demand will this profession be in the future? Is it easy to find a job in this field? How much money will I make?

These are important questions, and I will address all of them one by one in this series. But in my opinion, the real problem is that these questions are the **first** ones being asked.

## We pick the career and try to fit ourselves into it

From what I've seen—at least in Turkey—people generally pick a profession first, and then try to adapt themselves to it. Maybe it's similar in other countries, I wouldn't know.

We make our decision by looking at whether a career "pays better" or is "in higher demand." I think this is the wrong approach.

First, you need to know yourself. What do you love? What do you enjoy doing? How do you want to spend your time? Once you decide on these, you need to look at professions through that lens. In other words, getting to know yourself first and then turning what you love into your career makes much more sense.

## It's not easy, which is why you need to plan

Of course, this isn't easy. The answer to "What do I love doing?" doesn't become clear overnight. You need to plan for it.

The first step is to start by getting to know yourself: clarifying what you like doing and what brings you joy. I'll explain how you can do that in the next part of the series.

## It is never too late for anything

In my view, the most critical point here is this: choosing work that fits your character and your own profile, and planning it properly.

And it's never too late for that. People can switch careers or change jobs at any stage of their lives. I know it's not easy. But it's not impossible either. Many people have pulled it off. Why couldn't you?
