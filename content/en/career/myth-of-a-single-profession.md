People often hold the belief: "I will choose one profession, make my living from it, and all my income will come from this single job." In reality, that is not entirely true.

We don't have to stick to just one profession throughout our lives. We can acquire multiple professions and work more than one job at the same time.

## How is a profession acquired?

What do we typically do? Primary school, middle school, basic education; followed by high school, and then a two- or four-year university degree. After that, we start a job, and within 3–5 years, we become experienced in that field. In other words, a person usually acquires a profession around age 20–25.

If you can acquire a profession that comfortably, you can acquire a second one too.

## A second profession in a related field

Let me give an example: You graduated with a degree in mathematics or math education. While working in that profession, you could take classes and exams through distance learning or open universities on weekends and evenings, and earn a degree in civil engineering, where math is heavily used.

I am not saying it is easy. But when you already have a certain foundation and an interest in math, channeling that into a second profession might be a great fit for you.

## Parenting is also a profession

In my opinion, one of the best examples of this is being a parent.

Especially after having children, people spend, say, 8–9 hours of their day at work, focused on their job. In their remaining time, they take care of their kids. They raise them through each stage of life: infancy, childhood, adolescence... They both observe child development and teaching a child how to write, while actively shaping how a human being grows up.

When you think about it, this is practically like a second profession. A huge part of your time, effort, and energy goes into it.

## Getting stuck in a single profession

I think this is a point people often overlook: focusing exclusively on a single profession when building a career. Life is much broader. When choosing a career, you also need to realize that you can add other professions and new skills alongside it over the years.
