*This post is part of my "Before Choosing a Career" series on choosing a profession and career planning. All posts in the series: [Career](/tr/kariyer/)*

For those at the beginning of their careers or looking to change fields, I recommend working toward a clearly defined goal. Here, as someone who has hired people, I want to look at things from the other side of the table.

## Not experience, but genuine interest

Let me give an example: A fresh graduate wants to become a network engineer and work in networking.

If a company is hiring a new graduate, it shouldn't be expecting work experience anyway. But personally, I look at it this way: I expect the candidate I'm hiring to have some foundational knowledge. I don't expect deep knowledge, but **at least genuine interest**.

## How do you demonstrate interest?

If someone wants to become a network engineer, I think they should at least take a course on Udemy, go through it once or twice, and review it. If they want to be a systems engineer, they should take a few relevant courses. Maybe watch free videos on YouTube.

My advice is to use **different sources** as much as possible. I don't recommend learning exclusively from a single resource.

## Have something to put on your CV

Even if they are new graduates, they should make sure they have at least something to put on their resume. Saying "I completed this course" is a starting point, in my opinion. If there is an in-person or online course they can attend and afford, putting that on their CV is even better. Of course, not everyone has the budget for that, and I completely understand.

Saying "I want to work in this field, I've taken these specific courses, and I'm currently studying these topics" is, in my view, the best way for a fresh graduate to show that they genuinely want to develop themselves in that domain.

## One step further: a certification

Taking it one step further means getting a certification. If a student at the start of their career or a fresh graduate can write on their CV, "I studied for the CCNA exam, passed it, and here is my certificate," I think they have accomplished something significant. They will definitely find a job somewhere.

I'm giving this example from my own field, but the same thinking applies to other professions as well.

## Interview preparation

Another point is preparing for job interviews:

- Use AI tools to thoroughly practice the **questions that might be asked** for that role and your **answers** before the interview.
- Definitely learn about the company: What is the company, where does it operate, what does it do, how many years has it been around? Master this information.
