General Overview
#

Hello expat friends, those dreaming of settling in Germany, or those already living here! Today, I’ve prepared a golden guide for you, especially for those considering hitting the European roads with their own LPG-powered car from Turkey, filled with my personal experiences. If you, like me, think you can continue to enjoy the economic fuel advantage of Turkey in Europe when coming to Germany, let’s examine together how realistic this dream is.

In Turkey, LPG offers a very attractive alternative to gasoline and diesel prices. If your car runs on LPG, you enjoy covering long distances at much lower costs. This seems like a great budget advantage, especially for a journey of thousands of kilometers from Istanbul to Munich, doesn’t it? I set out with this thought. The decision to come to Europe with my 2020 model Honda Civic LPG-powered car was, initially, a completely logical choice. The tank capacity was good, the journey was comfortable, and most importantly, fuel costs were much lower. However, after hitting the road, I realized that this “LPG paradise” in Turkey can transform into a completely different world within European borders.

So, what’s the situation in Germany and generally in Western Europe? Unfortunately, don’t expect an LPG infrastructure as widespread and problem-free as in Turkey. While LPG (known as Autogas) is available at some stations in Germany, it’s not as common as in Turkey or Eastern Europe, and most importantly, the filling nozzles differ. For our target audience, Turks living in or planning to move to Germany, this situation can lead to many problems, from being stranded on the road to incurring unexpected costs. Your primary motivation for reading this article should be to complete your journey as efficiently and smoothly as possible, without encountering such unpleasant surprises. Come on, don’t make the same mistakes I did, and embark on your European LPG adventure fully equipped!

Step-by-step LPG Refueling Process and Obstacles You’ll Encounter
#

Refueling LPG in Turkey is as easy as it gets, but in some parts of Europe, it can become quite complicated. This section will detail the steps you’ll encounter and what to pay attention to, assuming you’re starting with zero knowledge.

Step 1: Route Planning and LPG Station Identification
#

Where: Before starting your journey, use mapping applications or websites that show LPG stations along your route. I strongly recommend using sites like LPG.eu or the “LPG station” filters in navigation apps. It might not be possible to find an LPG station in every city, or even on every highway. Especially in Germany, LPG stations are not on every corner like in Turkey, but rather in specific highway rest areas (Tank & Rast) or near industrial zones in cities.

How: When planning your route, try to maximize your LPG tank, especially in DISH-compatible countries like Bulgaria and Romania. This will save you time and gasoline when transitioning to Western Europe. For example, on my car journey from Istanbul to Munich, filling up the tank in Bulgaria and Romania gave me a great advantage.

How Long It Takes: Identifying stations and planning your route might take 1-2 hours before your trip, but this is much more valuable than spending hours searching for stations on the road.

Things to Note: Check if the information online is up-to-date. Some small stations might have closed or stopped offering LPG service. It can be helpful to search for the station’s name on Google Maps and check recent user reviews.

Step 2: Arriving at the Station and Nozzle Check
#

Where: In Germany, you can usually find LPG at major fuel chains like Aral, Shell, OMV, or at Tank & Rast facilities on the highway. However, this doesn’t mean it’s at every station. I had to visit many stations near Munich. For example, rest areas on the A8 highway or industrial zones around large cities are potential places to find LPG.

How: Station signs will say “Autogas” or “LPG”. When you get to the pump, the first thing you should do is compare the nozzle at the end of the pump with your car’s tank inlet. Most cars in Turkey have a DISH nozzle. If you see an ACME or Bayonet type nozzle at the pump, you’ll need to prepare your adapter.

How Long It Takes: Finding the right pump or checking nozzle compatibility at a station takes a few minutes. However, if you don’t have an adapter or it doesn’t fit, searching for another station can take hours.

Things to Note: While attendants at some stations might be helpful, you might experience communication difficulties if you don’t speak German. It can be useful to carry a note with basic German words like “Do you have an LPG adapter?” or “Autogas”.

Step 3: Using the Adapter and Refueling
#

Where: The “where” for this step is the LPG station you found.

How: If the pump nozzle doesn’t fit your car’s tank and you have an adapter with you, select the correct adapter and attach it to your car’s filling inlet. Then, secure the pump nozzle to the adapter. This process is usually done by screwing or with a locking mechanism. Ensure the connection is tight and there are no gas leaks. The refueling process is similar to Turkey; hold or lock the pump to fill gas.

How Long It Takes: Refueling with an adapter might take 1-2 minutes longer than usual due to attaching and detaching the adapter.

Things to Note: Be careful not to have gas leaks when attaching or detaching the adapter. Do not smoke or create sparks during the process. If the adapter doesn’t fit perfectly or there’s a leak, do not force it or try to refuel. This can be dangerous.

Step 4: Payment
#

Where: Payment is usually made at the cashier inside the station. In Germany, you can pay with cash or card.

How: While at many stations in Turkey, attendants also help with payment, in Germany, you refuel yourself and go to the cashier to pay. Simply state your pump number.

How Long It Takes: The payment process takes 2-5 minutes.

Things to Note: In Germany, some stations might not accept credit cards or might only accept German debit cards (EC-Karte). Therefore, it’s always a good idea to carry some cash with you.

#

Hidden Costs and Durations
#

Beyond official fuel prices, there are also “hidden” costs and time losses you might encounter with an LPG-powered car in Europe:

  • Adapter Set Cost: You’ll need to budget around 50-100 TL (average 2-5 Euro) for a quality adapter set purchased from Turkey. This is a one-time cost, but its absence can cause much bigger problems.
  • Wasted Fuel and Time: If you don’t have an adapter or go to the wrong station, the kilometers you cover searching for LPG will be spent on gasoline. This means both extra fuel costs and hours of lost time. Having to change stations multiple times, as I experienced in Budapest and Germany, can extend your planned travel time and increase your fatigue.
  • Stress and Demoralization: Encountering unexpected situations, especially in a foreign country, can raise your stress levels. This “hidden cost” can diminish the quality of your travel experience.
  • Towing Cost (Worst-Case Scenario): If you run out of LPG and your gasoline tank is also empty, you might have to call a tow truck. Towing services in Europe, especially on highways, are quite expensive (hundreds of Euros). Therefore, always make sure you don’t run out of fuel.

Total Estimated Cost and Duration: Considering the LPG price differences in Europe, coming to Germany with an LPG-powered car from Turkey will only provide significant fuel savings in the Eastern European section. Even if you find LPG within Germany, its advantage over gasoline is small, and it’s not as “cheap” as in Turkey. While you save in the first half of the journey (Bulgaria, Romania), in the second half (Hungary, Austria, Germany), your savings will either decrease or you will incur additional costs and time loss due to adapter search/gasoline use.

Practical Tips
#

Below you will find practical tips that I have personally experienced and that will be very useful for you to have a smooth journey with your LPG-powered car in Europe:

  1. Do Not Hit the Road Without an Adapter Set (Never!): This is the most important advice in this article. Absolutely get an adapter set that converts DISH to ACME and Bayonet from LPG accessory shops in Turkey (e.g., in Perpa or Ostim industrial zones) or online. Its price is nominal, but its value is priceless. I made this mistake, don’t you make it.
  2. Plan Your Route in Detail Before Setting Off: Especially identify LPG stations on your route after Hungary in advance. Applications like myLPG.eu or LPG.eu can show the location of stations and, in some cases, even nozzle types. Download maps in advance to use these applications offline.
  3. Keep Your Gasoline Tank Always Half Full or More: In case you can’t find LPG or experience adapter problems, make sure your gasoline tank always contains enough fuel. You might have to drive on gasoline for a long time in Germany.
  4. Learn Basic Fuel Terms: Knowing basic words like “Autogas” (German for LPG), “LPG”, “Benzin” (Gasoline), “Dizel” (Diesel), “Kredi kartı mı?” (Credit card?), “Nakit mi?” (Cash?) in German or English will make communication easier. Writing them on a small note or using a translation app is helpful.
  5. Carry a Small Amount of Cash: In Germany, some small stations or places off the highway might only accept EC-Karte (German debit card). Always having a few tens of Euros in cash with you can be a lifesaver in tight spots.
  6. Fill Up Your Tank at Border Crossings: Especially when crossing from DISH-compatible countries (Turkey, Bulgaria, Romania) to Western Europe, fill your tank at the last DISH station. This will give you a little more time in Western Europe before dealing with adapters or spending gasoline.
  7. Spare Insurance and Driving License Information: Be sure to check our articles Green Card Insurance Guide and Germany Driving License Renewal Process. Knowing the legal restrictions of living in Europe with a car from Turkey will prevent headaches in the long run. Learning the process you might experience with your driving license in advance will make your driving experience in Germany smooth.
  8. Long-Term Solution: German-Plated Vehicle: If you are living permanently in Germany, it is very difficult to continue legally and practically with your Turkish-plated LPG car for a long time. After approximately 6 months, you must return the vehicle to Turkey or convert it to a German plate, which is very costly and complicated. We also touch upon this issue in our article Germany Driving License Renewal Process. I will prepare a separate article about vehicle purchase-sale and registration processes in Germany.

Our Experience
#

When we set off for Germany with our 2020 Honda Civic LPG-powered car, I had great excitement and the thought, “how economical this journey will be!” We left Istanbul with a full tank, and in Bulgaria and Romania, we refueled LPG multiple times without any problems. Prices were close to Turkey’s, even lower in some places. Everything was going as planned, until we reached Hungary, specifically Budapest.

In Budapest, we visited a few stations, saw “Autogas” signs, and got our hopes up. But when we approached the pumps, we experienced a shock: the pump nozzle didn’t fit our car’s tank! I remember the helplessness of having to say “no” when station attendants asked in English, “do you have an adapter?”. At one station, they found an adapter suitable for the Hungarian standard, we tried it, but unfortunately, it didn’t fit our DISH type. That’s when I realized all my “economical journey” dreams had gone down the drain. We had to spend the entire rest of our route after Hungary, meaning Austria and Germany, relying on gasoline.

This situation unexpectedly increased our budget for the last 800-900 kilometers of our journey. Moreover, not just the cost, but our morale also suffered. The regret of “I wish we had brought that adapter with us!” gnawed at us. Seeing the LPG pump at every gas station but being unable to use it, having to continue to another station with the helplessness of not having an adapter, was truly exhausting. Especially in Germany, even though we could find LPG at large Tank & Rast facilities on the highway, not being able to use it due to the ACME nozzle was a huge disappointment.

This experience taught me a very important lesson: that not everything in Europe is “one standard,” and how critical detailed preparation is, especially for car journeys. I went through this experience so you don’t have to. An adapter set is a small cost but a gateway to great comfort and savings.

Unfortunately, at the end of this adventure, we had to sell our Honda Civic after returning to Turkey. As I also mentioned in our Green Card Insurance Guide, staying in Germany for more than 6 months with a Turkish-plated car is not practically and legally sustainable. This pushed me into the process of buying a new car in Germany, which is a whole other topic for another article…

Frequently Asked Questions
#

Below, I have tried to answer some questions you might have about LPG use in Europe:

  • 1. Can I permanently use my LPG-powered car brought from Turkey in Germany? No, you can use a Turkish-plated car in Germany for a maximum of 6 months. At the end of this period, you must either take the car back to Turkey or convert it to a German plate, which is very costly and complicated.
  • 2. Is LPG (Autogas) common in Germany? It is not as common as in Turkey or Eastern Europe. It can be found in major cities and at some stations on main highways, but not everywhere.
  • 3. How advantageous are LPG prices in Germany? While LPG is cheaper than gasoline in Germany, the price difference is quite small compared to countries like Turkey or Bulgaria. Significant long-term savings cannot be expected.
  • 4. Is using an adapter legal and safe? Yes, using the correct adapter set is completely legal and safe. The important thing is that the adapter fits perfectly into your car’s filling inlet and the pump nozzle, and there are no gas leaks.
  • 5. Can I buy my adapter on the road? It’s very difficult. Especially in Western Europe, your chances of finding a Turkish-type (DISH) adapter are almost non-existent. Therefore, it is essential to obtain it from Turkey before setting off.
  • 6. How can I find LPG stations? Websites and mobile applications like myLPG.eu or LPG.eu show LPG stations along your route, and in some cases, even nozzle types. You can also use the “LPG” filter in your navigation systems.
  • 7. Can I have an LPG conversion done on a car in Germany? Theoretically, yes, but LPG conversion costs in Germany are quite high and may have different standards compared to Turkish systems. Additionally, extra procedures related to your vehicle’s warranty status and technical inspections like TÜV may arise. It is generally not an economically sensible option.
